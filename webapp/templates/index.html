Central-server federated learning

Federated learning with a
quantum sense of agreement.

KIQFL (KAN-Enhanced Indirect Quantum Federated Learning) is a hybrid quantum–classical simulator for studying how clients can train locally while a central server aggregates updates. Circuit-state fidelity—optionally mixed by a ring DTQW and softened by a KAN—guides how much each client contributes. It is classical state-vector simulation for research, not a claim of QPU speedup.

What this system does

Run federated training with hybrid quantum–classical clients, compare algorithms side by side, and explore how sparse participation and simulated noise affect accuracy, loss, and fidelity.

🖥️

Central server FL

Star topology end to end: broadcast the global model, train on private data, upload updates, aggregate on the server, repeat.

🌀

Quantum-walk guidance

A discrete-time walk on a ring mixes circuit states used only to form fidelity weights—not a peer-to-peer parameter exchange.

🧩

KAN soft mixing

A small Kolmogorov–Arnold network sets a soft mixing strength α from noise, fidelity, and training progress.

⚛️

Hybrid CNN–QNN

Classical features feed amplitude encoding and a parameterized circuit; measurement probabilities drive the classical head.

🛡️

NISQ-aware stress tests

Sporadic client updates and configurable error rates help probe robustness under imperfect device conditions.

🔒

Data stays local

Raw samples never leave the client. Only parameters—and optional circuit states—reach the server.

Architecture at a glance

Clients keep private datasets. The server owns the global model. The ring walk is a guidance mechanism around that hub—not a replacement for it.
